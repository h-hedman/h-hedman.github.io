University of Illinois — Spatial Evaluation of Disease Management Program Implementation

Served as biostatistical lead on a large, multi-year evaluation of a state-run disease management and surveillance program. The work focused on identifying spatial and temporal heterogeneity in risk, measuring intervention effectiveness, and translating analytical findings into actionable guidance for policy and resource allocation.

Analysis was conducted using longitudinal surveillance data spanning more than a decade across multiple geographic jurisdictions.

What I Led

  • Biostatistical leadership: Led study design and analytical strategy for a $250K+ externally funded intervention evaluation.
  • Spatial risk assessment: Developed spatial and spatiotemporal models to identify non-random clustering and changing risk profiles over time.
  • Program evaluation: Quantified how intervention intensity and timing influenced downstream risk, enabling comparison across regions and periods.
  • Policy translation: Delivered findings directly to state partners, informing updates to intervention prioritization and management guidance.

Analytical Capabilities Applied

  • Spatial and spatiotemporal modeling
  • Cluster detection and anomaly identification
  • Longitudinal surveillance analysis
  • Intervention effectiveness evaluation

This work demonstrates how spatial analytics can be used to evaluate real-world intervention programs and guide policy decisions under uncertainty.

Methods: spatial statistics, scan statistics, longitudinal analysis, program evaluation
Tooling: R, GIS tooling, statistical modeling pipelines
