U.S. Department of State — Performance Modeling for Global Health Funding Allocation

Served as a data scientist and econometric analyst supporting global health funding allocation within the U.S. Department of State. Work focused on evaluating performance, efficiency, and equity considerations across large-scale international assistance programs operating in resource-constrained settings.

Analyses informed funding decisions affecting national health systems in multiple low- and middle-income countries.

What I Led

  • Econometric modeling: Developed and reviewed econometric analyses used to guide allocation of multi-million-dollar annual funding across three countries.
  • Performance evaluation: Assessed program outcomes against predefined KPIs to support evidence-based budget planning and prioritization.
  • Decision support: Translated analytical results into briefing materials for policy and program leadership.
  • Secure environment: Conducted analysis in a security-cleared setting with sensitive data and strict governance requirements.

Analytical Capabilities Applied

  • Econometric modeling and causal inference
  • Budget optimization and resource allocation analysis
  • Program performance measurement
  • Cross-country comparative analysis

In many low-resource settings, external health funding constitutes a substantial share of national health system capacity, requiring careful, data-driven allocation under uncertainty.

Affiliation: AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellow
Methods: econometrics, performance modeling, policy evaluation
Tooling: R, Stata, SQL, secure analytics environments
