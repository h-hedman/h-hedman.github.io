U.S. Department of State — Performance Modeling for Global Health Funding Allocation

Served as a data scientist and econometric analyst within the U.S. Department of State, supporting outcome-driven allocation of global health funding in low- and middle-income countries. Work focused on evaluating program performance, tradeoffs, and equity under severe budget and data constraints.

Analyses directly informed allocation of approximately $80M in annual funding across three countries, in contexts where external assistance represents a substantial share of national health system capacity.

What I Led

  • Outcome-driven econometric analysis: Led modeling efforts to evaluate HIV program performance and guide evidence-based allocation of constrained funding.
  • Spatial and comparative modeling: Applied spatial and econometric methods to assess intervention effectiveness and compare tradeoffs across regions and countries.
  • Decision support: Translated analytical findings into concise briefing materials used in interagency budget and policy discussions.
  • Automation and auditability: Built R and Python workflows to standardize and automate performance reporting, reducing analysis turnaround from hours to seconds while improving consistency and audit readiness.
  • Secure analytics environment: Conducted work in a security-cleared setting with sensitive data and strict governance requirements.

Analytical Capabilities Applied

  • Econometric modeling and causal inference
  • Resource allocation and budget optimization
  • Program performance measurement
  • Cross-country and regional comparative analysis

This work highlights how rigorous analytics can support high-stakes funding decisions where uncertainty, equity, and operational constraints intersect.
