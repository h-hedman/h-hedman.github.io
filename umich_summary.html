University of Michigan — Longitudinal Monitoring of Health Outcomes Across Human & Animal Populations

Led a multi-year, longitudinal research program examining health outcomes across interconnected human and animal populations in low-resource settings. The work integrated field-collected data, laboratory measurements, and statistical modeling to evaluate exposure pathways, risk propagation, and temporal change under substantial uncertainty.

Program execution required coordinated field operations, laboratory processing, and analytical workflows across multiple study sites and years.

What I Led

  • End-to-end program ownership: Designed and executed longitudinal observational studies spanning field sampling, laboratory processing, and analytical pipelines.
  • Team leadership: Directed and coordinated a multidisciplinary team of 15+ researchers, managing protocols, timelines, and data handoffs.
  • Funding and delivery: Independently secured and managed $100K+ in competitive research funding supporting field operations, laboratory analysis, and downstream modeling.
  • Data integration under uncertainty: Unified heterogeneous data sources (human, animal, environmental, molecular) into analyzable longitudinal structures.

Analytical Capabilities Applied

  • Longitudinal and hierarchical modeling
  • Spatial and spatiotemporal analysis
  • Measurement error and uncertainty handling
  • Integration of high-dimensional laboratory and field data

Findings from this work were disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and featured by the University of Michigan for institutional and public impact.

Methods: longitudinal modeling, spatial analysis, mixed-effects models, molecular and microbiological data integration
Tooling: R, Python, SQL, laboratory data pipelines, reproducible research workflows
